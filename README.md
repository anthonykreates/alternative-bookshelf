# The Alternative Bookshelf

A scrollable, physics-based digital bookshelf inspired by [Ben Tossell's bookshelf](https://bentossell.com/books/), featuring an expansive reading list of alternative and heterodox books across **sociology, psychology, history, anthropology, and systems thinking**.

The collection is primarily curated from **Venkatesh Rao's 17-year archive at [Ribbonfarm](https://ribbonfarm.com/bibliography/)**, alongside cross-referenced landmark reviews and recommendations from **Scott Alexander ([Astral Codex Ten](https://astralcodexten.com))** and **[Gwern Branwen](https://gwern.net)**.

---

## 📚 The 5 Curated Disciplines

1. **🏛️ Alternative & Subversive Sociology**
   - *Seeing Like a State* (James C. Scott) — State legibility vs. tacit knowledge (*metis*).
   - *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life* (Erving Goffman) — Impression management and social theater.
   - *Discipline and Punish* (Michel Foucault) — Panoptic surveillance and modern micro-power.
   - *The Civilizing Process* (Norbert Elias) — Manners and emotional suppression as products of state pacification.
   - *Images of Organization* (Gareth Morgan) — Corporations as psychic prisons and political systems.
   - *The Society of the Spectacle* (Guy Debord) — Hyper-mediated commodity representation.
   - *Amusing Ourselves to Death* (Neil Postman) — Huxleyan entertainment as censorship.
   - *The True Believer* (Eric Hoffer) — Fanaticism as an escape from personal inadequacy.
   - *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (David Graeber) — How violence created debt before money.
   - *The Human Condition* (Hannah Arendt) — Labor vs. work vs. authentic civic action.
   - *Bullshit Jobs* (David Graeber) — Managerial feudalism in knowledge work.
   - *Class* (Paul Fussell) — Micro-signals of caste and anxiety in modern society.

2. **🧠 Heterodox Psychology & Philosophy of Mind**
   - *The Origin of Consciousness in the Breakdown of the Bicameral Mind* (Julian Jaynes) — Auditory hallucinations as ancient gods.
   - *The Master and His Emissary* (Iain McGilchrist) — The takeover of Western civilization by the left hemisphere.
   - *Finite and Infinite Games* (James P. Carse) — Playing to conclude vs. playing to perpetuate play.
   - *The Denial of Death* (Ernest Becker) — Civilization as an immortality project against mortality terror.
   - *Thinking in Systems* (Donella Meadows) — Feedback loops, delays, and leverage points.
   - *Micromotives and Macrobehavior* (Thomas Schelling) — How innocent individual choices create unintended macro-results.
   - *Metaphors We Live By* (George Lakoff & Mark Johnson) — Embodied metaphor as the engine of logic.
   - *The Righteous Mind* (Jonathan Haidt) — Moral intuition as driver, reasoning as press secretary.
   - *The Nurture Assumption* (Judith Rich Harris) — Peer groups and genetics vs. parental style.
   - *Surfing Uncertainty* (Andy Clark) — The brain as a predictive processing engine.
   - *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (Douglas Hofstadter) — Strange loops and self-referential consciousness.
   - *The Blank Slate* (Steven Pinker) — Challenging the tabula rasa and noble savage dogmas.

3. **🗿 Deep Anthropology & Culture**
   - *The WEIRDest People in the World* (Joseph Henrich) — The Catholic Church's cousin-marriage ban and Western psychology.
   - *The Secret of Our Success* (Joseph Henrich) — Cumulative cultural evolution vs. individual IQ.
   - *Things Hidden Since the Foundation of the World* (René Girard) — Mimetic rivalry and the scapegoat mechanism.
   - *The Art of Not Being Governed* (James C. Scott) — Upland Zomia and deliberate anti-state evasion.
   - *Against the Grain* (James C. Scott) — Early grain-states as fragile, disease-ridden servitude traps.
   - *Albion's Seed* (David Hackett Fischer) — Four British folkways that define American regionalism.
   - *The Interpretation of Cultures* (Clifford Geertz) — Thick description and webs of significance.
   - *The Mushroom at the End of the World* (Anna Tsing) — Multispecies survival in capitalist ruins.
   - *Purity and Danger* (Mary Douglas) — Dirt as "matter out of place" and symbolic boundary policing.
   - *Cows, Pigs, Wars, and Witches* (Marvin Harris) — Ecological and economic drivers of religious taboos.
   - *The Dawn of Everything* (David Graeber & David Wengrow) — Seasonal political experimentation in ancient societies.
   - *Legal Systems Very Different From Ours* (David Friedman) — Stateless and customary dispute resolution.

4. **⏳ Macro-History & Epistemology**
   - *The Collapse of Complex Societies* (Joseph A. Tainter) — Diminishing marginal returns on bureaucratic problem-solving.
   - *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (Thomas Kuhn) — Normal science, anomalies, and paradigm shifts.
   - *Against Method* (Paul Feyerabend) — Epistemological anarchism: "anything goes" in science.
   - *Laboratory Life* (Bruno Latour & Steve Woolgar) — The social fabrication of scientific facts.
   - *Plagues and Peoples* (William McNeill) — Micro-parasitism shaping global history.
   - *The Transformation of the World* (Jürgen Osterhammel) — Standardization and global infrastructure of the 19th century.
   - *War and Peace and War* (Peter Turchin) — Frontier *asabiya* (cooperation) and elite overproduction cycles.
   - *The Great Cat Massacre* (Robert Darnton) — Mentalités and foreign mindsets in historical records.
   - *The Ancient City* (Fustel de Coulanges) — Greek and Roman domestic ancestor-worship cults.
   - *The Great Transformation* (Karl Polanyi) — The myth of the disembedded self-regulating market.
   - *Silencing the Past* (Michel-Rolph Trouillot) — Power and the production of historical narratives.
   - *Montaillou* (Emmanuel Le Roy Ladurie) — The daily lives and heresies of 14th-century Pyrenean peasants.

5. **🌐 Systems, Cybernetics & Subversive Strategy**
   - *Antifragile* (Nassim Nicholas Taleb) — Systems that gain from randomness and volatility.
   - *The Strategy of Conflict* (Thomas Schelling) — Focal points, self-binding commitments, and deterrence.
   - *The Cybernetic Brain* (Andrew Pickering) — British cybernetics and performative brains.
   - *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (Gregory Bateson) — Organism-plus-environment and double binds.
   - *The 48 Laws of Power* (Robert Greene) — The timeless, amoral mechanics of institutional courtiers.
   - *Rules for Radicals* (Saul Alinsky) — Asymmetric leverage against bureaucratic opponents.
   - *The Gervais Principle* (Venkatesh Rao) — Sociopaths, the Clueless, and Losers in modern corporate hierarchies.
   - *Tempo* (Venkatesh Rao) — Decision-making under radical uncertainty using narrative momentum.
   - *The Whole Earth Catalog* (Stewart Brand) — Access to tools and ecological cybernetics.
   - *Skin in the Game* (Nassim Nicholas Taleb) — Risk symmetry as the antidote to institutional fragility.
   - *Escape from Freedom* (Erich Fromm) — Alienation driving voluntary submission to demagogues.
   - *Simulacra and Simulation* (Jean Baudrillard) — The hyperreal territory pre-empting reality.

## 🚀 How to Run & View

### Option 1: Direct File Opening (No server needed)
Simply open `index.html` in your web browser:
```bash
# On Linux:
xdg-open /home/anthony/repos/alternative-bookshelf/index.html
# Or open directly in Firefox / Chrome:
google-chrome /home/anthony/repos/alternative-bookshelf/index.html
```
*(The app includes built-in fallback dataset loading, so it runs completely offline with no CORS warnings!)*

### Option 2: Local HTTP Server
```bash
cd /home/anthony/repos/alternative-bookshelf
python3 -m http.server 8080
```
Then navigate to: `http://localhost:8080`

## 🎮 Navigation & Interactions

- **Drag the Shelf**: Click and drag horizontally with momentum/inertia physics (just like Ben Tossell's shelf).
- **Trackpad / Mouse Wheel**: Scroll horizontally or vertically to pan along the shelf.
- **Scrubber Bar**: Click or drag across the bottom timeline to instantly scrub to any discipline.
- **Category Filter Pills**: Click any discipline (Sociology, Psychology, Anthropology, History, Systems) to focus and slide right to it.
- **Live Search**: Type in the search box to highlight matching books and dim non-matching ones across the entire shelf.
- **Inspect a Book**: Click any spine to open the popup detail card with the "Alternative Angle" takeaway, curator quote, and direct links.
- **Keyboard Shortcuts**:
  - `←` / `→` arrow keys: Jump between discipline sections.
  - `Escape`: Close open detail card.
  - `Tab` / `Enter`: Keyboard accessible navigation along all spines.
- **Theme Toggle**: Switch between warm library parchment (light) and late-night study room (dark) mode.
